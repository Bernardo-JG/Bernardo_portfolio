The prototype

I built a Wear OS application to explore heart-rate estimation directly from a watch’s raw PPG signals. It acquires green, infrared and red channels through Samsung’s Health Sensor SDK, displays the green waveform and estimates BPM on the watch.

The application combines sensor permissions and connection handling with a Compose interface and timestamped CSV recording. Raw signals are saved for offline inspection, making it possible to examine the data behind the displayed number.

From PPG to BPM

The pipeline assumes 100 Hz sampling and skips the first two seconds before processing. Stateful Butterworth filters apply a 0.5 Hz high-pass and a 4 Hz low-pass to the green channel, removing baseline drift and attenuating higher-frequency noise.

A five-second window advances every second. An adaptive peak detector identifies candidate pulses; valid intervals are restricted to 40–200 BPM, and the median interval gives the heart-rate estimate. A check against the previous estimate favours consistent intervals while allowing a fallback when all are rejected.

The waveform shows raw green PPG, while BPM comes from the filtered signal. The CSV records all three raw optical channels and timestamps. Separating acquisition, filtering and estimation made the processing choices explicit and available for further experimentation.

Comparison with the Galaxy Watch

In informal personal testing, I compared my estimates with the Galaxy Watch’s built-in BPM readings and observed broadly similar values in the situations tested. This was a useful practical check of the custom processing pipeline.

The comparison was qualitative: there is no published paired-reference error analysis or evaluation across participants and exercise conditions. Motion, watch contact and the assumed sampling rate remain limitations. The current prototype has no dedicated motion-artifact rejection and records while the activity is running.
